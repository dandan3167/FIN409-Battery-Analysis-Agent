# China’s Automotive Traction-Battery Industry, 2023–2025: A Five Forces Analysis with CATL as the Focal Company

## Introduction

China's vehicle traction-battery industry combines a rapidly expanding market with demanding conditions for retaining value. Reported domestic installations increased from 387.7 GWh in 2023 to 548.4 GWh in 2024 and 769.7 GWh in 2025. These observations establish growth in batteries installed in vehicles, rather than equivalent growth in manufacturers' revenue or profit. The central question is whether expanding demand gives producers durable bargaining advantages, or instead rewards continued investment while customers and competitors capture much of the benefit. [C001]

The relevant industry includes automotive batteries across chemistries, supplied externally or produced within vehicle groups. Stationary storage and starter-only batteries fall outside this boundary. CATL is the focal manufacturer, but its position must be distinguished from the industry's attractiveness. Its reported Chinese installation leadership and globally scoped traction-battery margins illuminate different aspects of its position; they cannot be combined into a Chinese unit price or a direct measure of domestic profitability. [C002] [C003]

Porter's Five Forces connects the evidence to pressures on value capture. The analysis weighs observable commercial mechanisms against counterweights, distinguishing reported outcomes from management explanations. It gives particular attention to qualification, customer development, supply continuity and alternative propulsion. Strong demand can accommodate more production without removing competitive pressure. Equally, a manufacturer can remain comparatively well positioned within an industry whose growth requires continual expenditure and whose returns differ substantially between firms. [C020]

## Rivalry among existing competitors

Rivalry is **High**, with low-to-medium confidence. The strongest evidence concerns order-related selling pressure and disclosed project pressures. TrendForce associated low-price selling in December 2023 with weak orders and suppliers' cash-flow needs, reporting category-average Chinese EV-cell price declines of 6–10%. This monthly category assessment does not measure matched-product price changes. Falling material costs offer an additional explanation. [C004]

EVE attributed affected project profitability in 2025 jointly to stronger competition and raw-material price fluctuations. Gotion reported mass-production deliveries of a jointly developed Volkswagen standard cell and design upgrades because previous specifications no longer matched a strategic customer's requirements. Delivery demonstrates an established competitor's commercial progress; specification changes establish technical renewal requirements. Neither observation independently establishes high rivalry. Project disclosures do not isolate a monetary loss caused by rivalry or establish Chinese delivery volumes for those projects. [C005]

Counterweights matter. Installation growth creates room for suppliers to expand without taking every additional order from one another. CATL retained a reported 43.42% installation share in the 2025 ranking. Nevertheless, that ranking's denominator differs from the overall installation series, and strict cross-year consistency remains unresolved. Leadership therefore supports CATL's commercial relevance without demonstrating an absence of competitive constraint. [C001] [C002]

Financial results further challenge a simple story of universal margin compression. CATL disclosed traction-battery margins of 23.94% in 2024 and 23.84% in 2025. EVE reported 15.50% in 2025, while Gotion reported 14.39%. These are globally scoped product businesses with different mixes. CATL's directly reported 2024 improvement was 5.81 percentage points on its comparative basis; the original 2023 margin cannot be spliced into that comparison. [C003] [C006]

High rivalry is retained because the order-related selling assessment and EVE's explicit competition attribution outweigh growth and margin resilience in this qualitative balance. The IEA's broader pack-price decline provides auxiliary context, including storage and imports. Technical renewal is an additional operating requirement, not decisive proof of intensity. Confidence is constrained by a single monthly observation, selected projects, mixed scopes and alternative causes. CATL's leadership supports response capacity without proving cost leadership or pricing power. Matched-product prices and comparable domestic returns remain missing. [C007] [C020]

## Bargaining power of buyers

Buyer power is **Medium**, with medium confidence. Vehicle manufacturers possess credible ways to influence battery suppliers through specification choices, supplier development and, in some cases, internal production. Gotion's Volkswagen standard-cell delivery demonstrates that an established alternative can progress beyond an announcement. BYD describes autonomous development and production of core components, including batteries, while retaining an open supplier ecosystem. Together, these facts make buyer alternatives commercially plausible, although neither establishes instant replacement of CATL on the same platform. [C005] [C008]

CATL reports sales-rebate arrangements in some customer contracts, establishing contractual variable consideration. The disclosure does not establish buyer-imposed concessions, who determined the terms, or their economic magnitude. Rebates may reflect volume incentives, ordinary commercial practice or a competitive response. They receive no independent weight as proof of buyer power; the judgment rests on credible alternatives balanced against implementation dependence. [C009]

The main counterweight is the work required to turn a battery source into a dependable vehicle programme. CATL describes customised or jointly developed product designs and long-term relationships involving equity participation, joint ventures and licensing. The inference is mutual dependence: adaptation and development may make abrupt replacement costly for both sides. Relationships do not, by themselves, prove exclusivity or contractual lock-in, and the customer descriptions also cover activities outside China's automotive market. [C010]

Moreover, technical standardisation can work in the opposite direction. CATL's account of products with short development cycles and low adaptation costs suggests that some solutions ease adoption rather than necessarily deepen switching barriers. A buyer may gain flexibility between programmes while remaining constrained during production of an existing model. The package does not measure programme-specific requalification time or cancellation costs. [C011]

Automaker-backed production adds a further option, but its maturity matters. Aion's timeline records YinPai's factory commissioning in December 2023; commissioning alone does not establish sustained deliveries or displaced external procurement. This event is shared with the entry discussion rather than counted as another independent pressure. [C012]

Medium intensity follows from the combination of real alternatives and bilateral implementation constraints. CATL should remain exposed to negotiation over future programmes even where current development relationships provide protection. The evidence supports selective buyer leverage, not an industry-wide ability to switch freely or dictate prices. The absence of comparable procurement contracts limits confidence in how that leverage translates into retained margins. [C021]

## Bargaining power of suppliers

Supplier power is **Medium**, with low-to-medium confidence. Materials remain economically important, while qualification can prevent frictionless replacement of particular suppliers. CATL describes formal assessment and selection of qualified sources, and cooperation covering raw materials and equipment. The commercial mechanism differs by input: commodity exposure can move costs without any individual vendor gaining negotiating control, whereas validated materials or specialised equipment may restrict immediate alternatives. The evidence does not quantify these constraints for the whole Chinese industry. [C013]

CATL's battery-industry direct-material cost share declined from 76.48% in 2024 to 71.79% in 2025, but expenditure increased from approximately RMB 202.723 billion to RMB 221.153 billion. A falling percentage therefore does not demonstrate falling expenditure or supplier dependence. This cost boundary includes more than traction batteries and remains auxiliary to the target industry. Volume, mix and other cost components could explain the divergent movements. Original amounts and exact conversions are retained in supporting records. [C014]

Resource concentration supplies a different warning. The IEA reports that the average share of the leading refining country, excluding rare earths, increased from 70% in 2023 to 72% in 2025. This is global country concentration, not concentration among CATL's suppliers, and domestic access within a concentrated country can differ from an importer's exposure. It supports concern about supply resilience without proving a particular producer's power over Chinese battery manufacturers. [C015]

Counterweights are tangible but incomplete. CATL reports mining, investment cooperation, long agreements and hedging arrangements intended to manage supply and price risks. Its 2025 recycling disclosures include 210,000 tonnes processed, covering batteries and materials, and 24,000 tonnes of regenerated lithium salts. These are distinct measures, not lithium-metal output or a self-sufficiency ratio. Investments and construction also cannot all be treated as operating supply. [C016]

These measures can widen supply options and reduce disruption exposure, while still leaving manufacturers dependent on qualified external inputs. Long agreements need not fix prices; hedging manages exposure rather than demonstrating that vendors have lost bargaining power. Equipment relationships additionally involve technical compatibility, so mineral-market conditions cannot determine their bargaining balance automatically. [C013] [C016]

Medium intensity reflects persistent input dependence moderated by supply organisation and alternative sourcing mechanisms. Confidence is limited because much of the operational evidence concerns CATL, whose resources may exceed those of smaller producers. For CATL, the evidence indicates capacity to manage exposure, not proven insulation from scarcity rents. Input-specific concentration, substitution lead times and comparable contract terms could change the judgment. [C022]

## Threat of new entrants

The entry threat is **Medium**, with low-to-medium confidence. Barriers are substantial for an independent firm starting without production experience or customers, yet automaker backing and industrial cooperation create feasible alternative routes. CATL describes the multidisciplinary knowledge, process design, manufacturing and quality control needed for large-scale battery production. This is an incumbent's account of capability requirements, not an independently measured minimum entry cost. The underlying mechanism is converting finance into repeatable, safe output that customers will accept. [C017]

Other disclosures make the implementation problem concrete. Gotion describes project delays involving process upgrades, long procurement cycles for imported or customised equipment, and staged investment. Because Gotion is already established, this is evidence of execution difficulty rather than a new entrant's failure. CATL's warranty disclosure also shows continuing repair responsibilities after sales. A newcomer therefore needs organisational and financial endurance beyond commissioning a production line, although the package does not measure a representative entrant's cash requirement. [C018]

Backing can relax several barriers simultaneously. An automaker may contribute a prospective customer relationship and application knowledge, while licensing or joint production can provide access to manufacturing capabilities. CATL itself reports using joint factories and technology licensing alongside its own plants. Such arrangements demonstrate available organisational routes; incumbent capacity expansion through them remains principally rivalry unless a genuinely new competitive supplier is established. [C019]

YinPai offers a more direct example of automaker-supported production. Aion records the factory's completion and commissioning on 12 December 2023. This supports the feasibility of reaching an operating milestone with vehicle-group support. It does not establish sustained commercial scale, battery deliveries to particular models, or substitution for purchases from CATL. [C012]

The relevant barriers are consequently commercial and technical, rather than an invented universal licensing wall. The cited industry normative conditions explicitly describe themselves as guidance without mandatory administrative-approval prerequisite status. That statement does not establish that all other applicable requirements disappear. [C023]

Medium intensity gives weight to supported entry while recognising the difficulty of reliable scale. It is lower for an unsupported independent startup and more credible for a vehicle-backed organisation. CATL benefits from accumulated capabilities but faces potential future reductions in external demand. The observed commissioning event informs both entry and buyer options through one connected mechanism; it does not justify two quantified revenue losses. [C024]

## Threat of substitutes

Substitution pressure is **Medium**, with medium confidence. The relevant alternatives are ways of providing transport while reducing reliance on traction batteries. Combustion-powered vehicles remain a functional alternative, and hybrid configurations can shift how much propulsion comes from stored electricity. The force is therefore broader than competition between battery chemistries. LFP, ternary, sodium-ion and solid-state approaches remain within the battery industry's boundary, while charging and swapping primarily improve the conditions under which batteries are used. [C025]

The vehicle market shows substantial electrification without universal adoption. Reported NEV domestic sales represented 50.8% of domestic automobile sales in 2025. This combines passenger and commercial vehicles and refers to enterprise sales rather than consumer retail purchases. The remaining share cannot simply be labelled pure combustion, while NEVs cannot all be labelled battery-electric. Vehicle counts also provide no direct conversion into battery GWh. The observation establishes coexistence, not a measured rate of battery displacement. [C026]

Purchase-price evidence limits a claim that combustion alternatives possess a general affordability advantage. For 2024, the IEA finds that close to two-thirds of battery-electric cars sold in China were cheaper than their combustion equivalents under its segment-based comparison. The benchmark uses sales-weighted combustion prices, inflation adjustments and an adjustment for registration-tax exemption. It is neither a matched-configuration comparison nor a total-ownership-cost measure, and it cannot be extended to heavy trucks. [C027]

Commercial-vehicle adoption conditions remain differentiated. Yutong discusses range, charging, swapping and hydrogen infrastructure difficulties in its heavy-truck material. Its specific fuel-cell tractor also contains a traction battery, illustrating why hydrogen propulsion need not eliminate battery demand. BYD's plug-in hybrid description likewise retains a battery within the propulsion architecture. These examples establish configurations and adoption constraints; they do not quantify net industry demand lost to either alternative. [C028]

CATL's disclosed chemistry range and swapping activity suggest ways to serve changing applications and improve convenience. They do not establish independently verified performance superiority or a measured reduction in substitution. A broader product range can help the company participate in hybrid demand even if battery intensity differs from an otherwise comparable electric vehicle. Changes in vehicle size, range and usage could also alter battery requirements independently of propulsion substitution. [C025] [C029]

Medium intensity follows from viable external transport alternatives moderated by expanding electrification and batteries' continued role in several competing architectures. Infrastructure and use patterns make pressure uneven. CATL's breadth supports adaptation, while matched-use ownership costs and configuration-specific demand evidence remain necessary to assess the actual constraint on pricing. Public or shared transport receives no decisive weight because its net battery-demand effect is unverified. [C030]

## Conclusion

Overall, the industry is **moderately unattractive for sustained value capture**, despite strong demand growth. Order competition and buyer alternatives constrain returns, while technical renewal and supply organisation require continuing commitments. Qualification, joint development and difficult independent entry protect established participation, but do not eliminate bargaining pressure or guarantee returns. These protections are unevenly distributed between manufacturers. On balance, competitive pressure outweighs the protection available to a typical producer; opportunities remain for firms with established capabilities. This is a qualitative industry judgment, not an average of force ratings or a measured profitability forecast. [C020] [C021] [C022] [C024] [C030] [C031]

CATL occupies a strong relative position within these conditions. Its installation leadership, resilient disclosed traction margins and evidence of customer development and supply management support the capacity to respond. They do not establish domestic cost leadership or unrestricted pricing power. Its financial evidence is globally scoped, its relationships are not universally exclusive, and neither resource investment nor recycling establishes complete independence from outside supply. [C002] [C003] [C010] [C016]

CATL's ability to perform therefore exceeds what the overall attractiveness judgment implies for an ordinary participant. Industry-level confidence is low-to-medium because monthly and project evidence cannot establish uniform pressure, and comparable domestic returns remain missing. Confidence in CATL's relative position is medium, supported by observed leadership and margins, without a precise forecast of future returns. [C031]
